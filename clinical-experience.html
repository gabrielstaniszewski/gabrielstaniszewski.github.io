THE LAB · FIRST
Where sterility became second nature
It began at a bench, not a bedside. During an early research internship I learned the quiet discipline of the lab: aseptic technique, working inside the laminar-flow hood, keeping a cell culture alive and uncontaminated for weeks at a time. Pipetting under sterile conditions, running gels, reading the bands: this was where I first understood that in medicine, precision is a form of care. The habits I built here (never break the sterile field, respect every step) are the same ones a surgeon lives by.

THE THEATRE · HIGH SCHOOL
Shadowing surgeons, still in high school
While still a high-school student I was given a rare privilege: to shadow surgeons inside a working hospital. I scrubbed in on the periphery, stood at the edge of the operating theatre, and watched teams move with calm, rehearsed urgency: the anaesthetist, the scrub nurse, the surgeon’s hands. Seeing an operation from a few feet away, rather than in a textbook, is what turned an interest in the heart into a decision about my life. This is the room I intend to spend it in.

Training in Lublin
