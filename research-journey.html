MATHEMATICS IA APPLIED CALCULUS
Navier–Stokes and the narrowed artery
Applying the Navier–Stokes equations to simulate how arterial stenosis (a narrowing of the vessel) disrupts the smooth, laminar flow of blood. The model traces the increased resistance and altered flow downstream of the blockage, turning a clinical condition into something you can watch and predict on a screen. The paper that pointed the whole journey toward the heart.
“A simple narrowing of the arteries can alter the course of blood flow, with huge implications for health.”

Synthesizing aspirin above the boil
The world makes some 40,000 tons of aspirin a year, but are the temperatures used to do it actually optimal? This study synthesizes acetylsalicylic acid at and beyond the boiling point of the solvent, measuring how temperature drives the gap between theoretical and true yield, and questioning the conditions industry takes for granted.
The first synthesis of aspirin in 1897 laid the foundation of the modern pharmaceutical industry.

BIOLOGY IA · ALGORITHMS IN NATURE
A brainless organism solving an impossible network
Some problems are so hard that even the most powerful computers can't solve them exactly. The Steiner Tree Problem, an NP-hard question of connecting points with the shortest possible network, is one. This paper hands the problem to slime mould, a brainless organism that naturally grows efficient paths, and reads its living solution against the challenge of modernizing Poland's rail network.
“The United States was built by rail, and a nation competes by moving materials quickly and cheaply.”

EXTENDED ESSAY · BIOLOGY · 2024
Growing life under a foreign gravity
If we ever colonize another world, could our crops take root there? Using a clinostat to simulate the gravities of Mars and of zero-g, this essay measures how altered gravity affects the germination and root length of cress (Lepidium sativum): a small, careful test of whether life could be grown elsewhere in space.
Research question: how does simulated gravity affect the germination of seeds, measured by root length?

2025–26 ELISA · ANTIBODIES
Reading gastric cancer in a well-plate
In the laboratory of PhD Magdalena Staniszewska, a collaboration between SDS Optic and the Catholic University of Lublin (KUL), I joined the hunt for the molecular fingerprints of gastric cancer. Working with specific antibodies, I ran ELISA immunoassays to detect and quantify candidate biomarkers in patient-derived samples. The colour change in each well is a quiet signal: a tumour that might be caught earlier, and treated sooner.
Bench science with the patient always at the far end of the pipette.
2025–26 CONFOCAL · LIPID DROPLETS
Cholesterol hiding in the white of the eye
A second question took me to the microscope: how cholesterol is stored in the sclera (the tough white outer shell of the eye) inside tiny phospholipid droplets. Under confocal fluorescence I imaged and characterised these lipid reservoirs, probing a quiet corner of cholesterol biology that connects, in the end, straight back to the arteries I first fell in love with.
Where the same molecule that narrows an artery is caught in the act of storage.
